Complex numbers --- definition & notations¶

  • A complex number: z=x+iyz = x + \mathrm{i}yz=x+iy, with x,y∈Rx,y\in\mathbb{R}x,y∈R.

  • Real and imaginary parts: Re⁡(z)=x, Im⁡(z)=y\operatorname{Re}(z)=x,\ \operatorname{Im}(z)=yRe(z)=x, Im(z)=y.

  • Vector-like notation: z=(x,y)z = (x,y)z=(x,y)

  • Using polar coordinates: z=r(cos⁡θ+isin⁡θ)=reiθz = r(\cos\theta + i \sin\theta) = re^{i\theta}z=r(cosθ+isinθ)=reiθ

## Complex numbers --- definition & notations (continued)


- A complex number: $z = x + \mathrm{i}y$, with $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.

- Real and imaginary parts: $\operatorname{Re}(z)=x,\ \operatorname{Im}(z)=y$.

- Vector-like notation: $z = (x,y)$

- Using polar coordinates: $z = r(\cos\theta + i \sin\theta) = re^{i\theta}$

More definitions:

- *Modulus* or *absolute value* of z: $|z| = \mod z = r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$

- $\theta$ is *angle*, also *phase*, *argument*, *amplitude* of z


## Complex conjugate


- A complex number: $z = x + \mathrm{i}y$

- Complex conjugate: $\bar{z} = z^* =  x - \mathrm{i}y$

- $\bar{z} = r(\cos(-\theta) + i \sin(-\theta)) = r(\cos(\theta) - i \sin(\theta)) = re^{-i\theta}$

Complex Series¶

  • Convergence of series involving complex numbers is defined analogously to that of real numbers, i.e., the series is convergent if its partial sum Sn=Xn+iYnS_n =X_n + \mathrm{i}Y_nSn​=Xn​+iYn​ approaches a limit S=X+iYS =X + \mathrm{i}YS=X+iY as n→∞n\to\inftyn→∞.

  • Practically, it means that Xn→XX_n \to XXn​→X and Yn→YY_n \to YYn​→Y.

  • For complex-numbered series, an absolutely convergent series converges. Therefore, we can test the absolute convergence.

  • In the complex power series ∑anzn\sum a_n z^n∑an​zn, where zzz and ana_nan​ are complex numbers, we are interested in a disk of convergence, with its radius called the radius of convergence.

And solve them for sin⁡\sinsin and cos⁡\coscos:

Hyperbolic functions¶

Trigonometric formulas can be generalized for complex numbers z as:

sin⁡z=eiz−e−iz2i\sin z = \frac{e^{\mathrm{i}z} - e^{- \mathrm{i}z}}{2 \mathrm{i}}sinz=2ieiz−e−iz​
(14)
cos⁡z=eiz+e−iz2\cos z = \frac{e^{\mathrm{i}z} + e^{- \mathrm{i}z}}{2}cosz=2eiz+e−iz​
(15)

Rewriting them for pure imaginary z=iyz = \mathrm{i}yz=iy gives:

sin⁡iy=e−y−ey2i=iey−e−y2≡sinh⁡y\sin \mathrm{i}y = \frac{e^{-y} - e^{y}}{2 \mathrm{i}} =\mathrm{i}\frac{e^{y} - e^{-y}}{2 } \equiv \sinh ysiniy=2ie−y−ey​=i2ey−e−y​≡sinhy
(16)
cos⁡iy=e−y+ey2i=ey+e−y2≡cosh⁡y\cos \mathrm{i}y = \frac{e^{-y} + e^{y}}{2 \mathrm{i}} = \frac{e^{y} + e^{-y}}{2 } \equiv \cosh ycosiy=2ie−y+ey​=2ey+e−y​≡coshy
(17)

These functions are called hyperbolic sine sinh⁡\sinhsinh and hyperbolic cosine cosh⁡\coshcosh. Their definitions for all z:

sinh⁡z=ez−e−z2\sinh z = \frac{e^{z} - e^{-z}}{2 }sinhz=2ez−e−z​
(18)
cosh⁡z==ez+e−z2\cosh z = = \frac{e^{z} + e^{-z}}{2 }coshz==2ez+e−z​
(19)
