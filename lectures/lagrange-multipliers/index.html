Lagrange Multipliers: An Inclined-Plane Example

Purdue University
Course-material provenance

This page was migrated from the CHM 696 course materials developed by Jonathan Hood and Lyudmila Slipchenko. Equations and examples remain under editorial review during the Fall 2026 website migration.

Setup.¶

Consider a particle (block) of mass mmm moving in the xyxyxy--plane. The straight line (inclined by angle α\alphaα to the xxx--axis) is given by the holonomic constraint

ϕ(x,y)=y−xtan⁡α=0.\phi(x,y)=y - x\tan\alpha = 0.ϕ(x,y)=y−xtanα=0.
(1)

Take yyy positive upward and gravity g=(0,−g)\mathbf{g}=(0,-g)g=(0,−g). Assume no friction.

Kinetic and potential energies are

T=12m(x˙2+y˙2),V=mgy.T=\tfrac12 m(\dot x^2+\dot y^2),\qquad V=m g y.T=21​m(x˙2+y˙​2),V=mgy.
(2)

Form the augmented Lagrangian with multiplier λ\lambdaλ:

L=T−V+λϕ=12m(x˙2+y˙2)−mgy+λ (y−xtan⁡α).\mathcal{L}=T-V+\lambda\phi = \tfrac12 m(\dot x^2+\dot y^2)-m g y + \lambda\,(y - x\tan\alpha).L=T−V+λϕ=21​m(x˙2+y˙​2)−mgy+λ(y−xtanα).
(3)

Euler--Lagrange equations with multiplier.¶

Treating x,yx,yx,y as independent coordinates, the Euler--Lagrange equations give

ddt(mx˙)−∂L∂x=0⟹mx¨+λtan⁡α=0,ddt(my˙)−∂L∂y=0⟹my¨+mg−λ=0,\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot x)-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial x}=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m\ddot x + \lambda \tan\alpha = 0,\\[6pt] &\frac{d}{dt}(m\dot y)-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y}=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m\ddot y + m g - \lambda = 0, \end{aligned}​dtd​(mx˙)−∂x∂L​=0⟹mx¨+λtanα=0,dtd​(my˙​)−∂y∂L​=0⟹my¨​+mg−λ=0,​
(4)

supplemented by the constraint

y−xtan⁡α=0.y - x\tan\alpha = 0.y−xtanα=0.
(5)

The multiplier λ\lambdaλ represents the normal reaction force component conjugate to the constraint.

Eliminate λ\lambdaλ and obtain motion along the line.¶

Introduce the coordinate sss measured along the line (positive in the direction making angle α\alphaα with the xxx--axis). The unit vector along the line is

u=(cos⁡α,sin⁡α),\mathbf{u}=(\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha),u=(cosα,sinα),
(6)

so the position on the line is r=su\mathbf{r}=s\mathbf{u}r=su, hence

x=scos⁡α,y=ssin⁡α,x = s\cos\alpha,\qquad y = s\sin\alpha,x=scosα,y=ssinα,
(7)

and

x¨=s¨cos⁡α,y¨=s¨sin⁡α.\ddot x = \ddot s\cos\alpha,\qquad \ddot y = \ddot s\sin\alpha.x¨=s¨cosα,y¨​=s¨sinα.
(8)

From the xxx--equation:

mx¨+λtan⁡α=0⇒ms¨cos⁡α+λsin⁡αcos⁡α=0m\ddot x + \lambda\tan\alpha = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad m\ddot s\cos\alpha + \lambda\frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}=0mx¨+λtanα=0⇒ms¨cosα+λcosαsinα​=0
(9)

or

ms¨cos⁡2α+λsin⁡α=0.(1)m\ddot s\cos^2\alpha + \lambda\sin\alpha = 0. \tag{1}ms¨cos2α+λsinα=0.(1)
(10)

From the yyy--equation:

my¨+mg−λ=0⇒ms¨sin⁡α+mg−λ=0,m\ddot y + m g - \lambda = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad m\ddot s\sin\alpha + m g - \lambda = 0,my¨​+mg−λ=0⇒ms¨sinα+mg−λ=0,
(11)

so

λ=ms¨sin⁡α+mg.(2)\lambda = m\ddot s\sin\alpha + m g. \tag{2}λ=ms¨sinα+mg.(2)
(12)

Substitute (2) into (1):

ms¨cos⁡2α+(ms¨sin⁡α+mg)sin⁡α=0.m\ddot s\cos^2\alpha + (m\ddot s\sin\alpha + m g)\sin\alpha = 0.ms¨cos2α+(ms¨sinα+mg)sinα=0.
(13)

Divide by mmm and use cos⁡2α+sin⁡2α=1\cos^2\alpha+\sin^2\alpha=1cos2α+sin2α=1 to obtain

s¨+gsin⁡α=0,\ddot s + g\sin\alpha = 0,s¨+gsinα=0,
(14)

hence

s¨=− gsin⁡α.\boxed{\ddot s = -\,g\sin\alpha.}s¨=−gsinα.​
(15)

Thus the acceleration along the line (taking sss positive upslope) is −gsin⁡α-g\sin\alpha−gsinα, i.e. the particle accelerates downhill with magnitude gsin⁡αg\sin\alphagsinα.

Normal reaction (multiplier).¶

From (2)(2)(2) we get

λ=ms¨sin⁡α+mg=m(−gsin⁡α)sin⁡α+mg=mg(1−sin⁡2α)=mgcos⁡2α.\lambda = m\ddot s\sin\alpha + m g = m(-g\sin\alpha)\sin\alpha + m g = m g(1-\sin^2\alpha) = m g\cos^2\alpha.λ=ms¨sinα+mg=m(−gsinα)sinα+mg=mg(1−sin2α)=mgcos2α.
(16)

The actual normal force magnitude (geometric normal) is

N=λcos⁡α=mgcos⁡α,N=\frac{\lambda}{\cos\alpha}=m g\cos\alpha,N=cosαλ​=mgcosα,
(17)

which is the familiar normal reaction on an inclined line.

Summary of equations.¶

The constrained Euler--Lagrange system is

{mx¨+λtan⁡α=0,my¨+mg−λ=0,y−xtan⁡α=0.\begin{cases} m\ddot x + \lambda \tan\alpha = 0,\\[4pt] m\ddot y + m g - \lambda = 0,\\[4pt] y - x\tan\alpha = 0. \end{cases}⎩⎨⎧​mx¨+λtanα=0,my¨​+mg−λ=0,y−xtanα=0.​
(18)

Eliminating λ\lambdaλ and using the coordinate sss along the line yields

s¨=−gsin⁡α,λ=mgcos⁡2α,\ddot s = -g\sin\alpha,\qquad \lambda = m g\cos^2\alpha,s¨=−gsinα,λ=mgcos2α,
(19)

and the physical normal force on the block is N=mgcos⁡αN=mg\cos\alphaN=mgcosα.
